How Scores Are Calculated

Every candidate is scored out of a theoretical maximum of ~55 points. The score is a composite of recent batting performance, plate discipline, team offensive context, and lineup position.

Step 1 — Candidate Filters Applied before scoring

Players must pass both filters to even be considered. This weeds out part-time players and free-swingers.

Filter Threshold Why
Plate Appearances (last 7 days) ≥ 10 PA Excludes injured, benched, or too-small sample players
Strikeout Rate (last 7 days) < 25% K% High strikeout hitters make less contact and are riskier picks
Step 2 — Scoring Components
Component Max Points Formula / Condition Rationale
Batting Average ~40 pts typical BA × 100 Core signal. A .300 hitter scores 30, a .350 hitter scores 35. Uses last 7 days of stats (or full season fallback if season data isn't available yet).
Low Strikeout Rate +10 pts K% < 12% Elite contact hitters who rarely strike out are more consistent. Flat +10 bonus.
Hot Team +10 pts Team in top 10 for runs scored (last 7 days) Offenses run in streaks. A player on a team that's been scoring a lot is in a better run-producing environment.
Lineup Position +10 pts max max(0, (9 − avg_order) ÷ 8 × 10) Leadoff hitters get the most plate appearances per game. Bonus scales linearly from +10 (leadoff) down to 0 (9-hole). See table below.

Full score formula:

Score = (BA × 100)  +  [10 if K% < 12%]  +  [10 if Hot Team]  +  max(0, (9 − avg_lineup_pos) ÷ 8 × 10)

Note: The avg_order is computed from Statcast data over the past 7 days. It is the average of each player's first-appearance rank within their team's half-inning per game. Pinch hitters and late substitutes are excluded.
Step 3 — A-List vs B-List Split

After scoring, the top 20 candidates are split into two lists based on risk flags. A player can have a high score and still land on the B-list if a risk is detected.

List Criteria Action
A-List No risk flags Safe pick — recommended for Beat the Streak
B-List Left-handed batter Still a strong hitter but carries platoon risk depending on the opposing pitcher's hand

The B-list is not a disqualification — it flags players worth reviewing manually. A left-handed hitter facing a right-handed pitcher is actually a platoon advantage; the risk flag is a reminder to check [L] / [R] on the matchup before picking.
